Projects

  • Indus Script Sign Functions
    2024–present  ·  Indus script data science

    Key to deciphering the Indus script is identifying which signs are phonetic and which are logographic. I'm applying computation to this problem, using statistical and distributional patterns in the corpus to differentiate these groups.

    I described my methods and results in an IJCA paper and another now under review at Digital Applications in Archaeology and Cultural Heritage. The broader Indus work was supported in part by an Emergent Ventures grant in 2025.

    Published paper (IJCA, 2025) →

  • Semantic Roles in Indus Sign Clusters
    2025–present  ·  Indus script distributional analysis

    This Indus project, under the mentorship of Rajesh Rao (UW), aims to identify the semantic roles of Indus sign clusters.

    Our goal is to avoid making linguistic assumptions about the script. Instead, our analysis relies entirely on statistical and distributional evidence: where sign clusters occur, what they co-occur with, and how they pattern across the corpus. A chapter co-authored with Rao is forthcoming.

    In the press: “The Indus Code: Who is in the race to crack a 4,000-year-old script?” — The Indian Express →

  • Proto-Dravidian Lexical Database
    June–July 2025  ·  linguistics database

    At the Technische Universität Berlin, I built a structured database of reconstructed Proto-Dravidian vocabulary under the supervision of Andreas Fuls and Martin Kada.

    This work was conducted by compiling existing research and by using AI and computational tools to automate the process of reconstructing words. One book chapter documenting this project is forthcoming in January 2027.

  • The Evolution of Written Chinese, 1910–1945
    2024–2026  ·  digital humanities NLP

  • Provenance Analysis of the British Museum
    2026  ·  digital humanities LLMs

    While at the Stanford Humanities Institute, I began working on a project to analyze the provenance records of the British Museum to identify unfair colonial extractions.

    I scraped over 20,000 records and built an interactive map to visualize their extraction. The resulting paper has been accepted in Computational Humanities Research (CHR 2027), where I'll be presenting it in January 2027.
