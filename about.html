Bio

I'm a senior at Sidwell Friends School in Washington, D.C. My main interests lie in history, linguistics, and in using computational tools to study these fields.

Many of my projects have centered on the Indus Script. My first set of projects aim to identify sign functions, specifically the distinction between syllabic and logographic signs. My second project, under the mentorship of Rajesh Rao, focuses on the semantic roles of recurring sign clusters. I received an Emergent Ventures grant in 2025 to support these computational analyses.

Nearby to that is my work on Indian historical linguistics, especially the Dravidian language family, which I researched at the TU Berlin in 2025. Here I worked under the supervision of Andreas Fuls and Martin Kada to construct a structured Proto-Dravidian lexical database.

I also work on the history of written Chinese, and I've conducted a computational analysis on Chinese newspapers from the Republican Era. I do digital humanities research at the Mount Zion and Female Union Band Cemetery, where I build interactive maps and digital resources from archival material. My work here focuses on uncovering the history of these forgotten Black cemeteries in Georgetown. I'm also working on a project using LLMs to analyze the provenance records of the British Museum to identify instances of unfair acquisition.

Beyond research, I'm an avid runner and I run for the cross-country and track team at Sidwell. I play violin, particularly klezmer and bluegrass. I've been learning Yiddish. I also listen to a lot of ska and rocksteady and study folk music history, Woody Guthrie, and American labor culture. I'm a trivia enthusiast and I captain my school's It's Academic quiz bowl team. I'm also interested in Civil War history, especially military technology; Tibetan history during the period of de facto independence; and Ancient Indian history.
