Bio

I'm a junior at Sidwell Friends School in Washington, D.C., expected to graduate in 2027. I use computational tools to think about questions in language and history that don't get easier just because they're old: undeciphered scripts, reconstructed vocabularies, and archives that are too large to read in a conventional way.

A lot of my recent work has centered on the Indus Script, though it has split into two somewhat different projects: one on sign function, especially the distinction between syllabic and logographic signs, and another on the semantic roles of recurring sign clusters under the mentorship of Rajesh Rao. I received an Emergent Ventures grant in 2025 to spend more time on that work, and one paper from it is published with another under review at Digital Applications in Archaeology and Cultural Heritage.

Nearby to that is my work on Indian historical linguistics — especially the Dravidian language family — which led to a research summer at TU Berlin in 2025, where I worked under the supervision of Andreas Fuls and Martin Kada on a structured Proto-Dravidian lexical database.

I also work on the history of written Chinese and on digital public history in Washington, D.C., including a long-running collaboration with Mount Zion and Female Union Band Cemetery, where I build maps and digital resources from archival material.

Beyond research: I run cross-country and track (9th at the D.C. JV State Championships in 2024; varsity since 9th grade). I play violin — klezmer and bluegrass, sometimes together. I've been learning Yiddish. I listen to a lot of ska and rocksteady and read about folk music history, Woody Guthrie, and American labor culture. I captain my school's It's Academic quiz bowl team.
