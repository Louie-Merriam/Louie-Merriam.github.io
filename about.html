Bio

I'm a junior at Sidwell Friends School in Washington, D.C., expected to graduate in 2027. My research sits at the boundary between computational methods and the humanities — I'm interested in what happens when you bring data science and natural language processing to bear on old, hard, humanistic questions: ancient scripts nobody has decoded, languages reconstructed from fragments, historical documents that exist by the thousands but have never been read at scale.

My longest-running obsession is the Indus Script. I received an Emergent Ventures grant in 2025 to pursue computational approaches to its decipherment, and I've published one paper on the topic with another under review at Digital Applications in Archaeology and Cultural Heritage. Adjacent to that is my work on Indian historical linguistics — particularly the Dravidian language family — which led me to a research internship at TU Berlin in 2025, where I worked under the supervision of Andreas Fuls and Martin Kada to help build a structured database of reconstructed Proto-Dravidian vocabulary.

I also work on the history of written Chinese and on digital public history in Washington, D.C., including a long-running collaboration with Mount Zion and Female Union Band Cemetery, where I build GIS-based maps of burial and residential records drawn from historical archives.

Beyond research: I run cross-country and track (9th at the D.C. JV State Championships in 2024; varsity since 9th grade). I play violin — klezmer and bluegrass, sometimes together. I've been learning Yiddish. I listen to a lot of ska and rocksteady and read about folk music history, Woody Guthrie, and American labor culture. I captain my school's It's Academic quiz bowl team.
