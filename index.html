Hello! I'm a senior at Sidwell Friends School in Washington, D.C. I'm primarily interested in the digital humanities, computational linguistics, history, and data science. Most of my projects center on applying computational methods to questions in language and history, including but not limited to undeciphered scripts, reconstructed languages, and archives.

Recently, I've been working on the Indus Script, Dravidian historical linguistics, Republican-era Chinese newspapers, and archival history in Washington, D.C. I've worked at TU Berlin, Mount Zion-Female Union Band Society Cemeteries, and the New York Historical Society. My research has also been supported by an Emergent Ventures grant.

Outside of these projects, I run cross-country and track, play violin (with a passion for klezmer), and spend quite a good bit of time listening to ska, rocksteady, and Woody Guthrie. I've also been studying Yiddish over the past couple of years and have been compiling a collection of my favorite Yiddish curses and expressions.

Current work

  • Indus Script Sign Functions
    2024–present  ·  Indus script
    This project aims to differentiate Indus script signs that are syllabic (phonetic) from those that are logographic. The work is documented in a published paper and follow-up now under review at Digital Applications in Archaeology and Cultural Heritage. In the press: The Indian Express, “The Indus Code” →
  • Semantic Roles in Indus Sign Clusters
    2025–present  ·  Indus script
    Under the mentorship of Rajesh Rao, this project aims to identify what semantic roles recurring sign clusters serve, using only statistical and distributional evidence without linguistic assumptions or biases. A chapter co-authored with Rao is forthcoming.
  • Proto-Dravidian Lexical Database — TU Berlin
    2025  ·  linguistics
    At TU Berlin, I built a structured Proto-Dravidian lexical database under the supervision of Andreas Fuls and Martin Kada. One chapter describing this work is forthcoming in January 2027.
  • Mount Zion Cemetery — GIS & Digital Archive
    2024–present  ·  digital humanities GIS
    Archival research, GIS mapping, and public digital resources for Mount Zion and Female Union Band Cemetery. MZION/FUBS map → Wilberforce map →
  • The Evolution of Written Chinese, 1910–1945
    2024–2026  ·  digital humanities
    A newspaper-based study of how written Chinese changed between 1910 and 1945. Dataset published in the Journal of Open Humanities Data (2026); paper under review at Computational Humanities Research (CHR 2026). Research Square preprint →
  • Provenance Analysis of the British Museum
    2026  ·  digital humanities
    I used LLMs to analyze the British Museum's provenance records and identify unfair colonial acquisitions. I scraped over 20,000 records and built an interactive map to visualize their extraction. Paper under review at Computational Humanities Research (CHR 2026). Provenance map →
