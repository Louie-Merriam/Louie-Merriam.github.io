I'm a junior at Sidwell Friends School in Washington, D.C., working at the intersection of computational methods and the humanities. Most of my research applies data science and natural language processing to questions that historians and linguists have been wrestling with for a long time — ancient scripts, language change, the texture of archival sources.

My ongoing obsessions include the undeciphered Indus Script, the deep history of Indian languages, the evolution of written Chinese, and the computational tools we might use to get traction on all of it. I've been lucky enough to pursue these at TU Berlin and the New York Historical Society, and I received an Emergent Ventures grant in 2025 to support my work on the Indus Script.

Outside of research: I run cross-country and track, play violin in klezmer and bluegrass settings, and spend more time than I should listening to ska and rocksteady records and reading about Woody Guthrie. I speak some Yiddish.

Project highlights

  • Computational Decoding of the Indus Script
    2024–present  ·  comp. linguistics published
    Using distributional analysis and statistical methods to identify sign functions in the undeciphered 4,000-year-old Indus Script. Funded by Emergent Ventures. One paper published, and a second is under review at Digital Applications in Archaeology and Cultural Heritage.
  • Proto-Dravidian Lexical Database — TU Berlin
    2025  ·  linguistics forthcoming
    Built a structured database of reconstructed Proto-Dravidian vocabulary at TU Berlin under the supervision of Andreas Fuls and Martin Kada. One book chapter forthcoming, spring 2026.
  • Mount Zion Cemetery — GIS & Digital Archive
    2024–present  ·  digital humanities GIS
    Historical and legal research on property disputes involving formerly enslaved women in D.C., translated into interactive GIS maps from archival sources. Built and maintain the cemetery's digital archive and public webpages.
  • The Evolution of Written Chinese, 1910–1945
    2024  ·  digital humanities under review
    A computational study of Republican-era Chinese newspapers tracking how written Chinese changed as China modernized. Under review at the International Journal of Digital Humanities. Research Square preprint →
